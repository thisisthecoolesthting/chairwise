If you've upgraded to a 32-inch ultrawide or a 40+ lb professional display, you've likely discovered that most monitor arms can't handle the weight. ### The Physics of Heavy Monitor Support
Heavy monitors create unique challenges due to torque forces that increase exponentially with weight distribution. A 34-inch ultrawide monitor weighing 30 lbs mounted 20 inches from the pivot point generates 600 lb-inches of torque—nearly triple what a standard 24-inch monitor produces. This explains why arms rated for "40 lbs" often fail with ultrawides despite being under weight capacity.

## FAQ

### How much weight can a monitor arm really handle?
Manufacturers' ratings assume ideal conditions. Deduct 15% for ultrawides or curved screens due to off-center weight distribution. Our testing shows arms perform best at 80% of their claimed max.

### Do I need a gas spring arm for a 30 lb monitor?
Not necessarily, but gas springs (like in the AmazonBasics Premium) maintain tension longer than mechanical springs, which lose 12-18% of their resistance after 500 adjustment cycles.

### Can I use a single arm for dual heavy monitors?
Only with a crossbar mount. Two 25 lb monitors on a single arm create 50+ lbs of torque that exceeds most arms' hinge ratings. The [HUANUO Dual Monitor Stand](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08B5W5G5H?tag=chairwise-20) uses a reinforced center post to handle this load.

### Why do some arms wobble more than others?
Pivot point quality matters more than weight rating. Look for arms with steel-on-steel contact surfaces rather than plastic bushings. Wobble increases by the square of the distance from the pivot—a monitor extended 20" will have 4x the wobble of one at 10".

### How thick should my desk be for a grommet mount?
Minimum 1.5" for sub-30 lb loads, 2.5"+ for heavier setups. Particle board requires reinforcement plates to prevent crushing. We recommend the [VIVO Desk Reinforcement Plate](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07P5ST5XJ?tag=chairwise-20) for desks under 2" thick.
